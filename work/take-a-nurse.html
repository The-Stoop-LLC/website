TAKE A NURSE

Brand Identity System, Stationery & Guidelines

Author: Mike Pernice, Founder · Last updated: September 2026

Take a Nurse is a nurse-driven, patient-focused small business in Pittsburgh, PA that advocates for independent individuals who need help understanding and navigating their medical care. The Stoop designed its visual identity from the mark up — a logo system built around a stethoscope that forms the N and resolves into a heart, five approved color variants, a seven-color palette, a two-typeface type system, business cards, letterhead, and a brand identity guide — and is now building the website on the same system.

The Challenge

Take a Nurse grew out of founder Kathy Fadgen's own experience. Her out-of-town parents were going to medical appointments without her, and unknown treatment plans and medication changes led to stress, delayed care, and unnecessary emergency room visits. The brand had to feel like the service itself — a calm, trusted advocate — and it had to hold up everywhere a healthcare brand shows up: a website header, a business card, an embroidered uniform, a form, a phone screen. It also needed rules so it stays consistent no matter who produces the next piece. The Stoop delivered the full system: the mark, the lockups, the colors, the type, the stationery, and the guide that governs them.

Workstream 1 — Logo Design

The Logo System

Horizontal Lockup · Stacked Lockup · Logomark · 5 Color Variants

The identity is built around one idea: a stethoscope that draws the N and resolves into a heart. The Stoop delivered it as three lockups — a horizontal default for websites, email headers, and signage; a stacked version for square formats and event graphics; and a standalone logomark for avatars, favicons, and watermarks — each supplied in Primary two-color, Blue, Magenta, Black, and White, with clear-space and minimum-size rules so the mark survives embroidery, vinyl, and small screens.

Workstream 2 — Color & Typography

Palette & Type System

Blue & Magenta Lead · Norteans Display · Roboto Text

Blue carries trust and structure; magenta carries energy and warmth. Care Teal, Warm Gold, Cloud, and Ink support them, each with a defined role and hex, RGB, and CMYK values so print and digital match. Norteans handles headlines and key numbers; Roboto does the reading, with a recommended scale from H1 to captions and a misuse page that protects proportion, color, spacing, and contrast.
